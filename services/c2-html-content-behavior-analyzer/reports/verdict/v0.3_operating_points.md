## How the thresholds were chosen

On the 391 train+val pages' OUT-OF-FOLD fused probabilities (the 9A fusion strategy fitted on 4 folds, predicting the 5th), separately for every subset of arms (weights renormalized over the arms that answer):

- **Malicious** if P(benign) < t_m, t_m = the largest cut-off whose OOF benign FPR is ≤ 2 % (exactly Part 9A's operating point);
- **Suspicious** if P(benign) < t_s, t_s = the smallest cut-off at which ≥ 95 % of the OOF threat pages fall below it (never below t_m);
- **Legitimate** otherwise. risk_score = round(100 × (1 − P(benign))). The test pages were never used for any choice.

## Reading the numbers (viva)

- With 34 benign test pages one page = 2.9 % FPR; with 34 threat pages one page = 2.9 % recall. The OOF numbers (193 benign / 198 threat) are tighter.
- The Suspicious band trades benign FPR for recall by design (95 % OOF threat recall): a Suspicious verdict means 'check before trusting', a Malicious one 'block'.
- The evidence floor only ever raises verdicts; its cost is the benign pages listed above (rule evidence that also occurs on real sites).
- Parity ≈ 0 shows that the live engine reproduces the stored 9A numbers, i.e. the API serves the evaluated models.
